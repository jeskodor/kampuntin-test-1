Community science

Para-ecology: researching the forest from within the community

In Maikiuants, community members train as para-ecologists. They combine Shuar knowledge of the forest with scientific methods to document biodiversity and protect their territory.

What is a para-ecologist?

A para-ecologist is a community member trained in ecological monitoring methods. They have known the forest since childhood — the tracks, the calls, the plants — and learn to record these observations systematically, so they can be compared over the years and shared with researchers.

This way, knowledge stays in the community, and the data help to make decisions about the territory: where to hunt less, which areas to protect, how the river is changing.

Para-ecologist writing observations in a field notebook

What we monitor

Simple methods, applied consistently, that anyone in the community can learn.

📷

Camera traps

Automatic cameras on trails and at salt licks record large, elusive mammals such as jaguars and tapirs.

🥾

Fixed transects

Regular walks along defined routes to record tracks, droppings, calls and sightings.

🐸

Birds and amphibians

Dawn call counts and night searches for frogs, sensitive indicators of forest health.

💧

Water quality

Simple measurements in rivers and streams: temperature, turbidity and aquatic macroinvertebrates.

🌳

Trees and nursery

Recording seed trees, flowering and fruiting, linked to the plant nursery and agroforestry.

📖

Shuar knowledge

Shuar Chicham names, uses and stories about plants and animals, documented only with the consent of those who share them.

Researching in the Amazon?

We welcome universities, organisations and independent researchers who would like to collaborate. All research is agreed with the community beforehand and follows these principles:

  • Free, prior and informed consent of the community assembly.
  • Para-ecologists take part in the fieldwork and receive training.
  • The data also belong to the community and are returned in a format that is useful to it.
  • Traditional knowledge is never published without explicit permission.
