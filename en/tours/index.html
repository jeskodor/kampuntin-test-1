Tours · 10 days · max. 12 people

Ten days in the rainforest, as part of the community

Our tours are not a show: you share daily life in Maikiuants, walk through the forest with Shuar guides, work in the gardens, cook, listen to stories and discover a different rhythm of life.

  • 📅 10 days
  • 👥 Max. 12 people
  • 🛖 Stay in a traditional jea
  • 🍲 Shared meals
  • 🧭 Community guides

Travel slowly, truly live together

For ten days you live in Maikiuants, about two and a half hours from the nearest amenities. We sleep in a traditional jea, bathe in the river and eat together. The days combine forest walks, work in the nursery and gardens, crafts, cooking and free time.

Groups are small — twelve people at most — so that encounters stay personal and the community can welcome you without changing its way of life.

Visitors and community families cooking together

What your tour could look like

This programme is a guide. Weather, the river and community life set the pace.

  1. 1

    1. Arrival

    Meet in [meeting town], travel about 2.5 hours to Maikiuants and get welcomed by the community.

  2. 2

    2. Getting to know the community

    A walk through Maikiuants, the school and the family gardens (aja). In the afternoon, your first river bath.

  3. 3

    3. The healing forest

    Forest walk with a Shuar guide. Medicinal plants, useful trees and how to read animal tracks.

  4. 4

    4. Hands in the soil

    Morning in the plant nursery and agroforestry plots. Free afternoon to rest or play with the children.

  5. 5

    5. Clay and fibres

    Pottery with the women of the community and crafts made from forest materials.

  6. 6

    6. Excursion

    Full-day trip to [waterfall / viewpoint], with a leaf-wrapped lunch in the middle of the forest.

  7. 7

    7. Science in the forest

    With the para-ecologists, we check camera traps and learn how wildlife is monitored.

  8. 8

    8. Shuar cooking

    We prepare traditional dishes such as ayampaco and cassava chicha. Music in the evening.

  9. 9

    9. Minga

    Communal work together with the families, followed by a farewell celebration with music and songs.

  10. 10

    10. Departure

    Last breakfast together, goodbyes and the journey back to [meeting town].

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to be very fit?

Normal fitness is enough. We walk on muddy trails with climbs and river crossings. If you have any limitations, let us know and we will find alternatives.

Is there electricity, phone signal and internet?

A solar panel powers the kitchen. Phone signal and internet are limited or non-existent — a good chance to disconnect.

Which languages are spoken?

The community speaks Shuar Chicham and Spanish. [State whether the Kampuntin team accompanies groups in English or other languages.]
